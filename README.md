Log of one day's sailing. The Passage Post treats Tuesday
25 November 2026 as a voyage and keeps its log the way a ship
does: every entry timed, every position fixed, nothing entered
that was not seen or honestly reported by someone who was.
Thirty-seven stories ride this page, out of nine regions,
in deep sea blue and coral.

The log opens in the strait. Iran says three tankers were hit
by mines in the Strait of Hormuz — entered under CLAIMED,
because the log records the claim as a claim until the lane
itself confirms it. Next entry: Baghdad's order to seize the
Kurdish oilfields, an order about the cargo under half the
region's hulls. Third: a car bomb in Colombia, twenty-three
injured — a port where the day turned violent ashore.

The rest of the manifest, in working order. Early-warning jets
for Poland, sailing under NATO colours. A count of Russian war
dead past 700,000 — the heaviest single figure in the log.
Fifty Boeing jets on a Xiamen order. Twelve coral species
brought back on the Great Barrier Reef, entered by the science
wires and the happiest line in the book. Madrid's 5–1 comeback.
Brussels' new sanctions. Storms gathering over Thanksgiving
travel on both American coasts. Washington's list of seven
firms targeted over chip diversion. And the PCE report, due
this day, entered as DUE with its figure left blank — a log
does not invent a reading the instruments have not given.

Two entries carry dates that are not today, and the log says
so on their faces: Nvidia's rise is logged under MONDAY CLOSE,
because it belongs to Monday's sailing, and the rationing in
Iran is dated to the 22nd, when it began. Four more stories
were refused passage entirely and are listed in the HELD IN
PORT box: a Mercury mission dated the 23rd, climate talks
from earlier in the month, a Liverpool result from an earlier
round, and launch hype for a game with no release today.

The chart work is drawn for a reader standing at the rail.
The route map is pinned by card number, so any story can be
found where it happened. The ports-of-call column reads the
day as a route — departure, under way, landfall. The figures
along the top are the day's eight heaviest numbers, each
tagged with the card it was weighed on.

How the log was kept: `compose_passage.py` lays the page out
twice — a paper masthead over a wide lead, and a sea-band
masthead over three leads abreast — and the duel is kept with
the build notes. The first hand won: the strait deserved the
wide water. `main.py` carries the finished page to
`/output/entry.png` unchanged.
